A little about my approach

"I’m here to build confident problem-solvers who aren’t afraid to get it wrong the first time."

Middle and high school students often walk into math convinced you either "get it" or you don’t. My first job is to change that mindset. When teens feel respected and know their voice matters in the room, they’re willing to take intellectual risks. I pair strong relationships and clear routines with productive struggle—teaching students that a wrong answer isn't a dead end, it’s just data. Real success in my classroom looks like analyzing the misstep, adjusting the strategy, and having the grit to try again.

01 / About & Philosophy

People first.
Learning always.

My work in middle and high school education starts with a simple conviction: adolescents do their best thinking when they feel respected, challenged, and capable of figuring things out.

My background

A teacher, a learner, a builder

Over seven years at Diagonal Community School District, I’ve taught across secondary mathematics—from 6th grade math through Calculus I, including Statistics and integrated STEM—alongside K–12 computer science courses like Computer Science Principles, Computer Science A (Java), Web Design, and App Design. Alongside classroom teaching, I’ve served as Technology Director, State Testing Coordinator, Online Class Coordinator, and Live Stream Producer.

Teaching across middle and high school has shown me how much students need adults who combine high expectations with genuine encouragement. Whether someone is encountering formal algebra for the first time or debugging their first program, I want them to trade "I’m just bad at this" for the confidence to test an idea, see what happens, and try again.

Philosophy of education

Belonging makes growth possible

Relationships come first. I learn students’ names, stories, and what matters to them because trust is the foundation for meaningful learning. A structured, warm classroom—with clear routines, individual check-ins, and daily collaboration—helps students feel safe enough to ask questions and take on challenges.

Equity means holding high expectations while offering different ways to reach them. I meet students where they are, build on what they know, and use scaffolds, choice, and multiple representations to make rigorous learning accessible. Mistakes are useful information; reflection and feedback help turn them into next steps.

Center connection before curriculum

I intentionally invest in each student from day one, learning their names, stories, and motivations. By actively listening and validating what matters to them, I build a foundation of trust where students feel genuinely seen, respected, and supported.

Build routines that empower independence

I establish consistent, predictable routines from day one so students can focus their energy on creative and complex thinking. By co-creating expectations, modeling classroom workflows daily, and reinforcing procedures with clear visual anchors, I eliminate guesswork and create a calm space where students feel secure enough to take academic risks and thrive.

Equity by design

High expectations combined with flexible support ensure every student can succeed.

Growth through practice

Feedback, reflection, and productive struggle help students see progress in themselves.

03 / Classroom & Instruction

A classroom built for belonging

Warm relationships and predictable routines help students feel ready to participate, take risks, and learn together.

Classroom management

Structure creates room to grow

I build a classroom where students are known and expectations are clear. Consistent routines make it easier for everyone to focus on the learning, while check-ins and family communication help students feel supported.

10 minutes

Warm-up

Students begin with a focused prompt that activates prior learning and gets them thinking right away.

10 minutes

Direct instruction

A concise lesson introduces or connects the day’s key ideas and makes space for questions.

Then

Practice & check-ins

Students apply the learning, collaborate, and get feedback while I check in and adjust support.

Communication is part of the routine

I make time for individual check-ins and family communication so students have consistent support and a clear path to ask for help.

Instructional materials & strategies

Tools in service of understanding

Technology is most useful when it deepens understanding—not when it is simply something to click. I choose tools and activities to help students visualize, practice, discuss, and explain their thinking.
